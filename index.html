Hi, I'm Mine!

(Pronounced mee-neh.) I am a fifth-year PhD candidate in the Neuroscience and Cognitive Science (NACS) program at the University of Maryland, College Park, where I am advised by Dr. Rochelle Newman and Dr. L. Robert (Bob) Slevc.

I study how people learn structure from what they hear and see. My dissertation examines what properties of the input make statistical learning more or less efficient, comparing speech, non-linguistic human sounds, tones, shapes, and signs. I also work on music cognition, including how background music affects speech comprehension and whether dogs are sensitive to musical structure, as well as on language development in Turkish-speaking children.

Before UMD, I was the lab manager of the Language Learning and Meaning Acquisition Lab at Purdue University with Dr. Arielle Borovsky. I received my B.A. in Neuroscience and French from Vanderbilt University, where I was a research assistant in Dr. Lense's lab.

I expect to finish my PhD in summer 2027 and am looking for postdoctoral positions. Feel free to get in touch!
